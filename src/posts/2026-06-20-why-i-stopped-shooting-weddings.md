**Short answer:** If you're a creator with a 9-to-5 and you want to start earning online, don't aim to get rich. Aim for one week of groceries from your first client. Small, real money from a real person is the motivation that keeps you going, and it's exactly how I started.

My son Aaron was 9 weeks old when we took the camper on a road trip to Denmark. Walking on the beach, I started thinking about something and shared it with my wife.

## What most coaches tell you about beginners

Roughly 80 to 90% of the coaches online advise against working with beginners. Don't create content for people who are starting out, they have no money, they don't want to invest in themselves. Nobody says it that bluntly. It's wrapped in "choose your avatar right" and "your content needs to solve a problem for rich people."

My wife said, "Mhm, but everybody starts there, right?"

She's right. Most of us start from zero, except the people who inherit something. So why leave those people out?

## Why creators with a 9-to-5 get ignored

Coaches want to sell high ticket, a $5,000 or $10,000 program or a $500 monthly subscription. Beginners are skeptical of that, and honestly they have a reason: what if the promise isn't kept? I've been in $2,000 programs where it wasn't. It feels terrible.

This doesn't fit everyone, and there's no one-solution-fits-all. But I've seen that people who start with a small, useful product often come back for more. You can build a real living by serving people who are starting from zero, because I was one of them.

## My first money online was two days of groceries

The first money I made online was basically two days of groceries. It made me incredibly happy, because for the first time I didn't have to take that money out of my salary.

That became my motivation:

- Aim for a week of groceries.
- Then a month.
- Then buy my wife a gift with money the business made.
- Go out and pay with it.
- Put 30% of what the business makes aside and invest it.

I didn't get here by quitting my job in one dramatic move. I was scared to do it, and in the end I was laid off, which left me no other choice. I'm telling you this because I know the starting point, and I want to help if your goal is to build something of your own.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I make money online if I'm a beginner?
Yes. Start with a small, concrete goal such as a week of groceries from your first client, rather than trying to get rich. Selling to people who are also starting out is a real market.

### How much should my first goal be?
Small and real. My first goal was two days of groceries, then a week, then a month. Small wins keep you moving.

### Do I need to quit my 9-to-5 to start?
No. I didn't plan to leave mine. Build the offer and your first client next to your job, and decide about the rest later.

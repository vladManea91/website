**Short answer:** The way you market tells people who you are. If your marketing leads with fear and points out everyone's mistakes, you're teaching your audience to feel bad before they buy. You can sell just as well by supporting people and showing up for them.

Life has good parts and bad parts. This week we took my son Aaron to the doctor.

Three people had told us we should go, because his tongue tie was too far forward. That can make it hard to latch, and later can mean problems with teeth, the jaw and even pronouncing letters. We're fresh parents with barely a clue what we're doing, and on top of that comes this.

The appointment was at 11:00. We arrived on time and waited until 11:45. Aaron got impatient and woke up, and everything we'd planned fell apart. That's how it goes sometimes.

When we were called, the nurse asked how old he was and whether he was our first. She invited me to sit in a huge chair, and I put Aaron on the chair by himself. She said no, you hold him and sit. I told her, "You should let people know, it's the first time I'm doing this."

The doctor checked him and the tie needed to be cut. My wife was sent to the waiting room, two nurses and the doctor came in, and it took five seconds, but I had to hold him. He cried like crazy with blood on his face. I'm not sure who got more traumatized, him or me. I don't think you can prepare for something like that.

Yesterday we went for a two-hour walk so he could calm down, and while walking, random thoughts came.

## The marketing that made me unsubscribe

This week I cleaned up my newsletters and unfollowed a lot of huge creators. I can't take it anymore when emails come with subject lines like "one mistake you always make" or "you'll never get clients". The system behind them is always the same:

1. Lead with fear.
2. Point out the mistakes.
3. Ask people to buy your product.

While walking I thought: now that I have a kid, would I do that to my kid? Would I point out every mistake, undermine every action and lead with fear? I wouldn't. So I won't do it to my audience either. You're supposed to lift your audience up, support them and help them, not undermine what they do in order to make them buy.

## What this means for selling your first client

If you want your first online client, you'll have to talk about your offer. You can do that in a way that shows up for people and helps them, or in a way that points out every mistake until they feel they need you.

I choose to lead by supporting. People buy from someone who helped them and is easy to trust. That's also how we approach selling in the [Customer Unlock Sprint](/customer-unlock-sprint/): clear, honest offers and real conversations, with no fear tactics.

I want you to take one thing from today: decide what kind of marketing you want to do.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is fear-based marketing effective?
It can get clicks, but it can make people unsubscribe. I stopped following creators whose emails lead with fear and mistakes.

### How can I sell without being pushy?
Lead by supporting and helping your audience, make the offer clear, and let the people who need it say yes.

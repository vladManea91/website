**Short answer:** When your offer isn't selling, check traffic before anything else. Look at how many people actually saw the page where they can buy, and what share of them bought. In my case 80 visitors with a 5% conversion rate told me the offer was fine and I simply hadn't sent enough people.

I've been running my business since 2023, with ups and downs like everything in life. I invest in knowledge and I'm usually logical and solution oriented.

But I have no backup, no other job, only this business. And lately I made a big mistake: I ran it from fear and survival.

## What fear does to your decisions

I tried switching who I talk to in my content and it didn't work. My organic sales dropped, fear kicked in, and in that state you can't make decisions that help you. I forgot everything I knew. I went in circles asking why sales were so low and found no answer.

## The two numbers to check first

Then I helped a friend move flats, and while doing that I managed to step back. When sales go low, the first thing to check is traffic:

1. How many people landed on the page where they can check out?
2. What share of them bought?

I looked at my products. In the last month only 80 people had landed on the page where they could buy. The conversion rate was 5%. So conversion was good, and the only problem was that I hadn't sent enough traffic.

Then I looked at what I'd posted about that product: one to three pieces of content. Of course traffic was low. Everything made sense.

## What this means if you want your first client

If you're selling a service or a coaching offer, the same logic applies:

- If nobody sees your offer, nobody can buy it. Check how many people actually saw it.
- If people see it but don't buy, the offer or the page isn't clear enough.
- Post about your offer more than once. One or two posts isn't a campaign.
- Give people one clean place to go, so a "yes" doesn't get lost in the DMs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are my sales low?
Check traffic first. Count how many people landed on your checkout page and what share bought. If conversion is fine, you need more people seeing the offer.

### What is a good conversion rate?
In my case 5% on a checkout page was good. The point is to compare your own numbers, not to chase a universal benchmark.

### How much content do I need about one offer?
More than one to three pieces. When I posted that little about a product, traffic stayed low.
